# NNNN — <a claim, not a topic>

Date: YYYY-MM-DD. Status: proposed | accepted | superseded by NNNN.

A record is written to be read by someone who was not in the room, two years later, while deciding
whether to change this. If a sentence would not help that person, it does not belong here.

## Context

What is true today, and what does it cost. Name the surface a reader can check — a file, a command, a
behaviour — rather than describing an intention.

## Decision

The sentence a person can quote in an argument. If it needs a paragraph of qualification to be true,
the decision is not finished.

## Evidence

Three kinds, kept apart on purpose. Mixing them is how a guess ends up cited as a measurement.

### FACT

What was measured, and by what. One line per fact, with the command that produced it.

```
<command>
<what it printed>
```

### REPORT

What someone told us — a user, a platform document, an upstream maintainer. Say who, because a report
from the cluster admin and a report from a blog post are different quality.

### ASSUMPTION

What we are guessing, and what would prove it wrong. An assumption with no falsifier is a belief.

## Consequences

What changes in the code, the files, the promises, and the things people will now see. Include what
gets deleted: a decision that only adds is usually two decisions.

## What this does not promise

The boundary. Every product claim has one, and writing it down is what keeps the claim from growing in
the retelling.
